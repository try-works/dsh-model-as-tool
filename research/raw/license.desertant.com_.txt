<p class="docmeta">Version 1.0 · 3 July 2026</p>
<p>Desert Ant Labs builds AI models and SDKs that run on your users' devices, not in the
cloud. Drop them into your app with no server to run, no inference bill, and nothing
leaving the device.</p>
<ul>
<li>Free below <strong>100,000 monthly active devices per platform</strong>, for each model; a commercial
  license is required above that.</li>
<li>Source-available (not open source), so you can read and build on the source, and the
  license does not expire.</li>
<li>Credit Desert Ant Labs where your users can see it, with a short "Powered by Desert
  Ant Labs" line; see the <a href="https://license.desertant.com/attribution">attribution guide</a>.</li>
<li>You may not use the models, their outputs, or anything you log from them to train a
  model that competes with ours.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="3-free-tier-and-commercial-tier">3. Free tier and commercial tier</h2>
<p><strong>Free tier.</strong> Your use is free while it stays below <strong>100,000 monthly active devices

independently. A Model on iOS and the same Model on Android each have their own
100,000 MAD allowance, and different Models are counted separately.</p>
<p><strong>Commercial tier.</strong> Once your use of a Model crosses 100,000 MAD on a Platform, you
need a commercial license for that Model on that Platform. Contact

<p><strong>No splitting.</strong> Accounts and applications under common control count together
toward the threshold; splitting them to stay under it is a breach, and we may recover
the usage at the commercial rates that would have applied.</p>
<p><strong>Research and teaching.</strong> Use for non-commercial scientific research, teaching, and
academic publication is free and exempt from the MAD threshold. This does not extend
to deploying a Model in a commercial product or service, or using a Model to develop
one. If your work is published, please cite Desert Ant Labs and link to the relevant
Model.</p>
<h2 id="4-what-you-can-do">4. What you can do</h2>
<ul>
<li>Run the Models in your application and ship that application to your users.</li>
<li>Log and analyze your application's own behavior, performance, and quality.</li>
<li>Tune thresholds and parameters for your use case.</li>
<li>Build accessibility features and custom product behavior on top of the Models.</li>
<li>Use Outputs to build classifiers, filters, or embeddings used solely inside your
  own application and not distributed as a model.</li>
<li>Benchmark the Models to decide whether to use them.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="6-what-you-cannot-do">6. What you cannot do</h2>
<p><strong>Do not build a competing model.</strong> You may not use the Models, their Outputs, or
anything you log from them, including embeddings and evaluations, to train, fine-tune,

Bulk-extracting or systematically logging Outputs to build a Competing Dataset is the
same breach; ordinary product logging under Section 4 is fine. This covenant is a
contract obligation and survives termination.</p>
<p><strong>Do not redistribute the Models on their own.</strong> You may not sell, sublicense, or
distribute the Models or SDKs themselves, or a substantially unmodified derivative, as
a standalone product, model, SDK, or hosted service. Embedding them in your application
and shipping that application is what this license is for.</p>
<p><strong>Do not reverse engineer for extraction.</strong> Except to the extent applicable law gives
you a non-excludable right (such as decompilation for interoperability), you may not
reverse engineer the Models, or attempt model extraction, distillation attacks, or
weight recovery to reconstruct or replicate them.</p>
<p><strong>Respect safety models.</strong> For any Model whose purpose is safety (e.g. content

misrepresent its safety-critical outputs. Tuning thresholds for your product is fine.</p>
<p><strong>Do not tamper with the telemetry</strong> described in Section 7, or interfere with its
reporting.</p>

<h2 id="20-definitions">20. Definitions</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Model / Models</strong> – a Desert Ant Labs on-device AI model (audio, video, image, or
  text) published by Desert Ant Labs, including its weights, and each successor
  version.</li>
<li><strong>SDK</strong> – a Desert Ant Labs software development kit that loads and runs a Model.</li>
<li><strong>Platform</strong> – each operating-system family, and the web, on which you ship (for
  example iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, Linux, and web), each counted separately.</li>
<li><strong>Monthly Active Device (MAD)</strong> – a device (for the web, a browser) on which your
  application calls a Model at least once during a calendar month, counted once
  regardless of call volume, as measured by the SDK. Each device is counted
  separately: one person using two devices, or two browsers, is two active devices.</li>
<li><strong>Output</strong> – what a Model produces from your inputs in your application.</li>
<li><strong>Competing Model</strong> – an on-device audio, video, image, or text model that
  substitutes for, is substantially similar to, or competes with a Desert Ant Labs
  Model.</li>
<li><strong>Competing Dataset</strong> – a dataset assembled from the Models, their Outputs, or logs
  for the purpose of training a Competing Model.</li>
<li><strong>Derived Data</strong> – telemetry, statistics, and signal that Desert Ant Labs generates
  or derives from operation of the Models.</li>
<li><strong>Reverse Engineer</strong> – to decompile, disassemble, or engage in model-extraction or
