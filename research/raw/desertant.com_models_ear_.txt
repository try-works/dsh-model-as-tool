      <section class="section section--narrow rise d3" id="overview">
        <h2 class="section-title">Detect spoken language from 30 seconds audio.</h2>
        <div class="prose">
          <p>A voice note comes in and the transcriber is set to English. 30 seconds in, Ear says Portuguese, and the transcriber that runs next is the Portuguese one instead of an English one returning nonsense with confidence.</p>
          <p>Ear doesn't listen to the whole file. Ear finds the three thirty-second stretches that sound most like speech, skips the jingle and the silence, and names the language in 250ms. The answer comes with a confidence and a flag that says whether to trust it.</p>
          <p>When the top two candidates are too close to separate, Ear says so. You ask the user or fall back to a general model instead of transcribing in the wrong language. One SDK, every platform: Swift on Apple, Kotlin on Android, JavaScript in the browser and Node. Nothing is uploaded.</p>
        </div>
      </section>
      <section class="section section--perf rise d4" id="performance" aria-label="Performance">
        <h2 class="section-title">The right transcriber, 98.5% of the time.</h2>
        <p class="section-lede">On 162 recordings, 98.5% of the answers Ear marked reliable routed to the right transcriber; on 12 ordinary uploads and 10 rebuilt as podcasts, no confident answer was wrong.</p>
        <div class="perf-hero no-photo">
          <div class="stat-row">
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">98.5%</div><div class="stat-label">Routed correctly</div><div class="stat-detail">answers above the reliability threshold, 162 recordings</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">99</div><div class="stat-label">Languages</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">250ms</div><div class="stat-label">Per identification</div><div class="stat-detail">three thirty-second windows</div></div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <p class="perf-note">Measured end to end through the SDK and published on the <a href="https://huggingface.co/desert-ant-labs/ear" rel="noopener">model card</a>. Speech mixed under louder music is read correctly about 60% of the time.</p>

      <section class="section section--uc rise d4" id="use-cases">
        <h2 class="section-title">Use cases</h2>
        <div class="uc-figure-grid">
          <figure class="uc-figure">
            <img src="/assets/photos/ear-card-816.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" width="1088" height="1360">
            <h3>Detect spoken language from 30 seconds audio.</h3>
          </figure>
          <div class="uc-figure-col">
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Pick the right transcriber first</h3><p>A transcriber pointed at the wrong language returns nonsense with confidence. In a meeting recorder or a voice-notes app, run Ear on the first 30 seconds and start the transcriber, the captions, and the translation in the language actually spoken.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Sort an archive of recordings</h3><p>Run Ear over a library of voice notes, calls, or episodes in a Node script or a Mac app overnight, and every file has its language by morning. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is billed per file.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Route a call or a voicemail</h3><p>In a support app or a messaging product, name the language of an incoming call or a voicemail before it reaches a person. The call lands with someone who speaks it, or with the right speech model.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Caption in the browser</h3><p>A web video tool identifies the spoken language client-side, with the same SDK in the browser and in Node, and picks the caption language so the uploader never has to.</p></div>

      <section class="section rise d5">
        <h2 class="section-title">What the model does</h2>
        <ul class="features">
          <li>99 languages, from Afrikaans to Yoruba, including the major European, Asian, and African languages.</li>
          <li>Picks the three thirty-second windows that sound most like speech rather than reading by position, so a music intro or a silent stretch doesn't fool it. Choosing by position finds the language 4% of the time on a five-minute recording with speech in a tenth of it.</li>
          <li>Returns the language, a confidence, the ranked candidates, and an <code>isReliable</code> flag. The flag is false when the top two are too close to call, and false for Norwegian, Swedish, and Danish, which the model confuses with each other.</li>
          <li>Already-decoded audio at any sample rate works; a file URL works on Apple.</li>
          <li>The weights download on first use and are cached; construction does no work and starts no download.</li>
        </ul>

      <div class="caveats rise d6">
        <p class="model-caveat">Ear names the language; it doesn't transcribe. Speech under louder music is read right about six times in ten, and Norwegian, Swedish, and Danish are confused with each other, so Ear marks those unreliable by design.</p>
        <p class="model-caveat">Recordings shorter than 30 seconds get one window and a less certain answer. Treat an unreliable answer as a question for the user, not a transcriber setting.</p>
