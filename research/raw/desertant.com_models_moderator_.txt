      <section class="section section--narrow rise d3" id="overview">
        <h2 class="section-title">Flag nudity before upload or display.</h2>
        <div class="prose">
          <p>Questionable uploads never reach your servers, your CDN or a moderator's queue. Five separate detections (exposed nipples, genitals, buttocks, nudity and sexual activity) feed the score, and your policy decides which of them count.</p>
          <p>Every image Moderator trained on is licensed or synthetic, so it goes into a commercial product with no copyright exposure. The whole model is 9.7MB on Apple and 9.2MB elsewhere, so the check runs on the phone before the upload starts.</p>
        </div>
      </section>
      <section class="section section--perf rise d4" id="performance" aria-label="Performance">
        <h2 class="section-title">Catches 87.8% of NSFW images.</h2>
        <p class="section-lede">Catches 87.8% of NSFW images and passes 93.7% of safe ones at a 6.3% false-block rate, about half NudeNet's, from a 9.7MB on-device model.</p>
        <div class="perf-hero no-photo">
          <div class="stat-row">
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">87.8%</div><div class="stat-label">Recall</div><div class="stat-detail">NSFW caught</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">93.7%</div><div class="stat-label">Specificity</div><div class="stat-detail">SFW passed</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">9.7MB</div><div class="stat-label">On-device</div><div class="stat-detail">int8 Core ML</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">74%</div><div class="stat-label">Per video frame</div><div class="stat-detail">1 crop, 97% specificity; sampling frames recovers recall</div></div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <p class="perf-note">Internal evaluation on a proprietary held-out set, not a published benchmark. The headline numbers are the 8-crop pass, which is the one to run on a single image; for video, one crop per frame is cheaper and more precise per frame, and sampling across frames recovers the recall.</p>
      </section>
      <section class="section section--uc rise d4" id="use-cases">
        <h2 class="section-title">Use cases</h2>
        <div class="uc-figure-grid">
          <figure class="uc-figure">
            <img src="/assets/photos/moderator-card-816.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" width="1088" height="1360">
            <h3>Flag nudity before upload or display.</h3>

          <div class="uc-figure-col">
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Pre-upload filtering</h3><p>Screen a user's image on the phone before it uploads. Nothing questionable reaches your servers, and no one on your team has to look at the image to find out.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>A policy you control</h3><p>The <code>.standard</code> policy counts all five detections. <code>.allowTopless</code> leaves exposed nipples out of the score, for platforms whose rules permit them.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Warn at the picker, not after the post</h3><p>Score an image the moment a user picks it, and warn them on the spot. An hour-later takedown costs you a support thread.</p></div>

      <section class="section rise d5">
        <h2 class="section-title">What the model does</h2>
        <ul class="features">
          <li>Trained only on permissively licensed and synthetic images, with nothing scraped.</li>
          <li>Scores any image or video frame for nudity on the device, before upload.</li>
          <li>Five detection heads: exposed nipples, genitals, buttocks, nudity, and sexual activity.</li>
          <li>A policy decides what counts toward the NSFW score, from <code>.standard</code> (all five) to <code>.allowTopless</code> (excludes exposed nipples).</li>
        </ul>
      </section>
