      <section class="section section--narrow rise d3" id="overview">
        <h2 class="section-title">Catch hate speech before it posts.</h2>
        <div class="prose">
          <p>Toxic scores three things separately, so a product can treat them differently. Hate speech means public incitement to violence or hatred against a protected group. Abuse means insults, ranked by how severe they are. A threat means violence against a person or group.</p>
          <p>On Multilingual HateCheck, a public suite of hand-written examples, Toxic gets 85 out of 100 across the seven EU languages. Mistral's Shieldstral gets 79 and wants a 16GB GPU; the guard models sized for a phone get 65.</p>
        </div>
      </section>
      <section class="section section--perf rise d4" id="performance" aria-label="Performance">
        <h2 class="section-title">0.847 on real hate speech, from 80MB.</h2>
        <p class="section-lede">0.847 macro F1 on real Multilingual HateCheck across the seven EU languages it covers, from an 80MB model that runs on device. The closest guard model through the same harness is Mistral's Shieldstral-1.0-3B at 0.786, and it wants a 16GB GPU; the ones sized for a phone score 0.65.</p>
        <div class="perf-hero no-photo">
          <div class="stat-row">
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">0.847</div><div class="stat-label">Macro F1</div><div class="stat-detail">7 EU languages, shipped weights</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">0.786</div><div class="stat-label">Best baseline</div><div class="stat-detail">Shieldstral-1.0-3B, same rows</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">80MB</div><div class="stat-label">On-device</div><div class="stat-detail">Core ML; 159.6M params</div></div>
            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">26-30%</div><div class="stat-label">False flags</div><div class="stat-detail">clean text, 7 EU languages, default setting</div></div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <p class="perf-note">Measured on the weights that ship, not extrapolated from a training checkpoint; three runs of the same recipe average 0.834. The threshold is 0.40 for the hate score and 0.50 for the other two. Out of domain, on corpora with no shared lineage: 0.724 on textdetox across 5 languages and 0.658 on offenseval2020 across 3. The seven EU languages are scored on real Multilingual HateCheck; the other 15 are scored on synthetic sets, because HateCheck has no real set for them, and those figures are development estimates rather than comparable scores. Latency has not been measured yet.</p>
      </section>
      <section class="section section--uc rise d4" id="use-cases">
        <h2 class="section-title">Use cases</h2>
        <div class="uc-figure-grid">
          <figure class="uc-figure">
            <img src="/assets/photos/toxic-card-816.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" width="1088" height="1360">
            <h3>Catch hate speech before it posts.</h3>
          </figure>
          <div class="uc-figure-col">
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Warn before the message is sent</h3><p>Score outgoing messages on the device before they are sent, so hateful, abusive or threatening content can trigger an inline warning or a review path without ever reaching your servers.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Comment triage without infrastructure</h3><p>Run every incoming comment through Toxic locally in the client and send only the likely hate speech to a moderator queue. The rest never needs a human, and no comment costs a cloud call.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Moderate in 23 European languages</h3><p>One model file covers 23 languages, from English, Spanish, and Polish through Bulgarian, Estonian, and Irish. No per-language model, and no separate language-detection step.</p></div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </section>

      <section class="section rise d5">
        <h2 class="section-title">What the model does</h2>
        <ul class="features">
          <li>Three scores per text: hateful, abusive, threat.</li>
          <li>Ten target signals under the hate score: race, color, religion, descent, national or ethnic origin, sexual orientation, gender, disability, age, other.</li>
          <li>23 languages from one model file, with a recommended setting per language in the model metadata.</li>
          <li>Runs fully on device. No text leaves the device to be scored.</li>
        </ul>
      </section>


      <section class="section rise d5">
        <h2 class="section-title">Specs</h2>
        <dl class="specs">
          <div><dt>Languages</dt><dd>23, from English, German, and French through Greek, Lithuanian, and Irish</dd></div>
          <div><dt>On-device size</dt><dd>80MB Core ML (4-bit); 91MB LiteRT (int4); 100MB ONNX (browser)</dd></div>
          <div><dt>Model</dt><dd>XLM-R-base, 12 layers, 159.6M params</dd></div>
          <div><dt>Accuracy</dt><dd>0.847 macro F1 on the 7 EU languages in Multilingual HateCheck, shipped weights; 23 languages supported</dd></div>
          <div><dt>Platforms</dt><dd>iOS and macOS (Core ML), Android (LiteRT), browser (ONNX); weights on Hugging Face, not in the SDK yet</dd></div>
        </dl>
      </section>
      <div class="caveats rise d6">
        <p class="model-caveat">Toxic is a triage aid, not a verdict. Send flags to a human reviewer or a heavier local tier instead of auto-removing.</p>
        <p class="model-caveat">Toxic reacts to hateful words even when someone is quoting or condemning them, so counter-speech and news reporting can trip the flag. At the default setting it flags 26 to 30% of the non-hateful text in the seven EU languages it was measured on, and 12% in English.</p>
        <p class="model-caveat">Toxic separates hate speech from ordinary text well, and hate speech from plain rudeness much less well, so an angry customer can read as hateful.</p>
      </div>
