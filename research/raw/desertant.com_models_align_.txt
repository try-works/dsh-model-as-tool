      <section class="section section--narrow rise d3" id="overview">
        <h2 class="section-title">Accurate word timestamps for any transcript.</h2>
        <div class="prose">
          <p>Apple says "world" runs from 2.61 to 3.04 seconds. Align says 2.57 to 2.98. Cut there and the cut is clean, the caption lights up on the word, and the highlight starts where the speaker did.</p>
          <p>You keep Apple's transcriber. Align reads the same audio SpeechAnalyzer already gets and returns the same words with tighter start and end times, in a few milliseconds, from a 0.7MB download. On clean audio that takes Apple's average miss from 113ms to 45ms.</p>
          <p>A word Align can't improve keeps Apple's timing, so a correction can only help or leave the word alone. Nine languages, and a locale outside them passes through with Apple's timings untouched.</p>
        </div>
      </section>
      <section class="section section--perf rise d4" id="performance" aria-label="Performance">
        <h2 class="section-title">Five times more accurate.</h2>
        <p class="section-lede">Apple's word timings on LibriSpeech test-clean are off by 106.4ms on average; Align brings that to 20.2ms, and 95% of words land within 50ms, from a 0.7MB model.</p>
        <div class="perf-hero no-photo">

            <div class="stat"><div class="stat-value">5x</div><div class="stat-label">More accurate</div><div class="stat-detail">106.4ms to 20.2ms</div></div>

        <div class="panel board">
          <p class="board-caption">Mean absolute distance from the reference word boundary, on a 500-clip sample of each official LibriSpeech split, with no speaker shared between training and evaluation</p>
          <div class="table-wrap" tabindex="0"><table class="table"><thead><tr><th>Split</th><th class="num">Apple raw</th><th class="num">Align</th><th class="num">Within 50ms</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">test-clean</th><td class="num">106.4ms</td><td class="num">20.2ms</td><td class="num">95%</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">test-other</th><td class="num">111.6ms</td><td class="num">24.8ms</td><td class="num">92%</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

        <p class="perf-note">Evaluated on the v1.0.0 weights. The references are machine forced-alignment estimates from Qwen3-ForcedAligner averaged with a second aligner, not human annotations, so the figures show a large and consistent reduction of the timing error rather than sample-accurate ground truth. The one exception is the comparison against WhisperX, which is scored on 258 word boundaries a person corrected by hand against the waveform.</p>

      <section class="section section--uc rise d4" id="use-cases">
        <h2 class="section-title">Use cases</h2>
        <div class="uc-figure-grid">
          <figure class="uc-figure">
            <img src="/assets/photos/align-card-816.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" width="1088" height="1360">
            <h3>Accurate word timestamps for any transcript.</h3>
          </figure>
          <div class="uc-figure-col">
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Word-by-word captions that keep up</h3><p>Light up each word as it is said in a captions view, a karaoke-style lyric screen, or a language-learning app, from Apple's transcript. The timings land on the word instead of a tenth of a second off it.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Cut a clip on a word</h3><p>Trim a recording to a quote in a podcast editor or a video app and have the cut start where the word starts. No frame of the previous word, no clipped consonant, and no manual nudge on the timeline.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Highlight the spoken word in a transcript</h3><p>Scroll a transcript in step with playback in a meeting recorder or a lecture app, with the highlight moving on the word rather than drifting ahead of the audio.</p></div>
            <div class="use-case"><h3>Search that lands on the moment</h3><p>Jump to the second a word was said in an archive of recordings, on the device. The timings are tight enough that the playhead starts on the word and not in the middle of the previous one.</p></div>

      <section class="section rise d5">
        <h2 class="section-title">What the model does</h2>
        <ul class="features">
          <li>Corrects the word-level timings Apple's <code>SpeechTranscriber</code> and <code>SpeechAnalyzer</code> return, without replacing them: same words, same result surface, tighter <code>audioTimeRange</code> values.</li>
          <li>Mean timing error from 124.2ms to 43.9ms, macro-averaged across all nine languages so no single language carries the figure. On English it goes further: 106.4ms to 20.2ms.</li>
          <li>On a 500-clip sample of LibriSpeech test-clean, 95% of words land within 50ms of the reference, up from 37%. The worst tenth improves most: from 230.7ms to 33.0ms, about one frame of 30fps video.</li>
          <li>A structural fallback keeps Apple's original timestamp whenever a correction would be invalid, hits the search window's edge, or lacks streaming context.</li>
          <li>Nine languages: English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, German, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese. Other locales pass through unchanged.</li>
          <li>About 0.7MB of compiled Core ML in two stages, run on the CPU and Neural Engine; a typical result is refined in a few milliseconds.</li>
        </ul>

        <h2 class="section-title">Specs</h2>
        <dl class="specs">
          <div><dt>Accuracy</dt><dd>20.2ms mean error on LibriSpeech test-clean against Apple's 106.4ms, and 43.9ms across all nine languages against Apple's 124.2ms</dd></div>
          <div><dt>On-device size</dt><dd>About 0.7MB compiled Core ML (two 0.3MB stages plus the filter bank and calibrator)</dd></div>
          <div><dt>Languages</dt><dd>English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, German, Japanese, Korean, Chinese</dd></div>
          <div><dt>Model</dt><dd>Two-stage coarse-to-fine cascade over a log-mel spectrogram, with a gradient-boosted calibrator</dd></div>
          <div><dt>Platforms</dt><dd>iOS 26, macOS 26, tvOS 26, visionOS 26 on Core ML, plus Linux, Windows, and Node on LiteRT</dd></div>
        </dl>
      </section>
      <div class="caveats rise d6">
        <p class="model-caveat">Align corrects a transcriber's timings; it doesn't transcribe. The StreamingRefiner that attaches to Apple's SpeechAnalyzer needs iOS 26, macOS 26, tvOS 26, or visionOS 26.</p>
        <p class="model-caveat">The offline Align.refine corrects any transcript's words on its own, and runs on Linux, Windows, and Node through LiteRT.</p>
        <p class="model-caveat">Align can't promise to improve every word. The fallback that keeps the original timing catches corrections that look unsafe, not every wrong one.</p>
        <p class="model-caveat">Spoken numbers are the weakest case: on a small sample, refinement moved digit boundaries further from the reference than leaving them alone.</p>
